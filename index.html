Challenge

Adults under 35 avoid talking about money because it feels embarrassing, so they avoid dealing with it. A launch year had gotten them talking. Year two had to get them banking, with a goal of 10% more digital account openings.

How we did it

We took banking to Twitch and Discord, where gamers feel anonymous and comfortable. BMO streamed anonymous banking live with expert advice. Other BMO business lines sponsored streams for investing, small business and student products. More than 100 hours of searchable clips became an always-on financial library, supported on Twitter and TikTok.

Results

  • +15%digital account openings among under-35 gamers, against a 10% goal
  • +24%student account openings
  • +40%unaided awareness
  • 8.8Mvideo views

Challenge

Consumers criticize brands for rainbow-washing, putting rainbows on logos without real support. BMO's Rainbow Deposits web app lets anyone photograph a rainbow and deposit it, and BMO donates $1 to Rainbow Railroad for each one. In 2024 Pride felt less present, and BMO needed a fresh reason to keep people depositing.

How we did it

Pride Placements put custom out-of-home ads next to existing rainbows, such as crosswalks, flags and planters, so those rainbows became media people could deposit. Meta, TikTok and Snapchat flights were timed to each market's Pride parade, and drag queens and 2SLGBTQ+ influencers showed how to deposit. A drone show turned the CN Tower into a deposit target with a QR code, and a live Rainbow Deposit Box showed donations as they happened.

Results

  • +1,293%donations year over year during Pride weekend
  • $50,000donated to Rainbow Railroad in 2024
  • 3 Goldsat the 2024 Media Innovation Awards

The problem it solves

Research can be overwhelming, and strategists can freeze under it. The agent gives me a streamlined starting point for a specific trend, so I spend my time on the thinking instead of the hunting.

What it gives me

Statistics, consumer survey results, market trends and research insights from trusted sources, a fact-check of every number, and a one-page point of view with sources, all in one dashboard.

Why I built it

Every strategy starts with the same slow first day: collecting the numbers that make the case.

The need

In strategy work, the first step is gathering credible data on a trend: how big it is, who cares, and where it is going. Done by hand, that took 1-2 days before any real thinking started, and it is easy to get stuck reading instead of deciding.

The design choice

Code checks, and the model writes. Quotes are copied word for word by code, the model adds only one sentence of insight per quote plus the final report, and code then looks up every number in the quotes. Sources come from an allowlist that includes Statista, McKinsey, Nielsen, Ipsos and StatCan.

The result

I get a defensible starting point in about 314 seconds, with every fact tied to a source. It does not replace the strategy. It gets me to the strategy faster.

Finding the bottleneck, and fixing it

The first version took too long and pulled in old and irrelevant data. I tuned it with AI-assisted coding. Looking at where the time went showed that the slow part was the free reasoning model, not my code. A run made 7 AI calls one after another, and each took 20 to 30 seconds.

Fixes applied to the agent and why
What I changedWhy it helps
Ran independent work in parallel The 4 searches and the insight batches do not depend on each other, so they run at the same time. Seven waits in a row became about three rounds.
Cut stages the code already covers Code does the fact-check, so the Editor and Revision AI steps added the least. I dropped them and turned the planner into simple code, which leaves about two AI calls.
Used fewer sources and shorter outputs The agent keeps the top 8 trusted sources instead of 14, so there is less to read, less to write and less noise.
Built for free models It retries three times, retries an empty reply with a larger token budget, and skips and notes a failed source, so one outage does not end a run.
